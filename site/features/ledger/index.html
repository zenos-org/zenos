Features / Tamper-evident ledger

Working todayProved in Lean 4

A record nobody can quietly rewrite, not even the PC's own administrator.

Every open, release and key issue joins a hash chain. Every 30 seconds it is sent to audit nodes outside the PC, run by different organizations. They refuse a rewritten past and compare their copies with each other.

between shipments to the audit nodes

About 100 to 1

ordinary edits are anchored as one Merkle root, so the record stays small

How it works

Record, ship, compare

1

Record

Two tiers: every edit in a fine-grained chain on the PC, and the critical events plus a periodic anchor on the consortium.

  • Rebuilding the local chain cannot match anchors already sent
2

Ship

Batches are signed with the PC's root-only key. A node accepts only records that continue the chain it holds, and signs a receipt.

  • A changed past record, a gap or a bad signature is refused and alerted
  • A PC that goes silent is reported after 10 minutes
3

Compare

Nodes run by different organizations, such as ministries and an independent audit body, compare copies every few minutes. A PC that told them different stories shows up as a fork on both sides.

Not a cryptocurrency

No coins, no mining and no fees. The consortium is a fixed set of organizations that each keep a copy, so nobody, including the PC's administrator, can change the past alone.

Proven, and what is not

What the check guarantees

Proved in Lean 4

Checked on every build

  • The check accepts exactly the chains whose links and hashes hold
  • An audit node's copy stays intact and only grows
  • Two histories that pass the check and end at the same hash are the same history
Trusted, not proved

Tested and listed openly

  • A record can still be changed in the seconds before it is shipped
  • A PC kept offline ships nothing, though it is reported as silent
  • The consortium assumes most of its members are honest
  • The PC's audit key is a root-owned file, not yet in a TPM
